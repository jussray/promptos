# Project Sovereignty Contract

PromptOS must preserve prompt, skill, workflow, instruction, and governance meaning if an external model, provider, API, SDK, connector, runtime, sibling project, or vendor disappears or materially changes.

## Invariants

- **Human authority comes first.** Authorized humans are the root of consequential authority. Models, agents, workflows, registries, evidence, learned knowledge, fingerprints, proof cookies, and automations may inform or constrain a proposal but may not manufacture, renew, widen, or replay authority.
- Human authority does not rewrite reality. Approval can authorize an action; only evidence can support a truthful execution or outcome claim.
- Workflow names, modes, prompts, skills, and registry membership are instruction/control semantics, not authority tokens.
- Project-owned contracts define canonical inputs, outputs, errors, authority, state, evidence, workflow meaning, and completion semantics.
- External models/providers and sibling projects are adapters/capabilities, not PromptOS identity.
- Critical dependencies are `REPLACEABLE`, `DEGRADED_FALLBACK`, `HARD_DEPENDENCY`, or `UNKNOWN`.
- Provider/model loss may reduce capability but must not erase workflow meaning, evidence requirements, registration authority, version truth, or recovery paths.
- Provider swaps must not broaden tool permissions, replay stale approvals, duplicate mutations, or weaken evidence requirements.
- Provider-specific results map into project-owned receipts; provider acceptance is not founder-goal outcome proof.
- When operated through Founder Control Room, PromptOS remains a sovereign subsystem and portable instruction/governance layer, not a competing founder operating system.

## World as experience and knowledge

Legitimate real-world interaction may become evidence-bearing experience: founder/operator reports, workflow outcomes, repository history, provider/runtime results, experiments, evaluation results, operational failures, documents, and verified outcomes.

```text
World → observation → provenance → evidence classification → experience receipt → knowledge candidate → contradiction / red-team check → context + confidence + freshness → working knowledge → proposal → authorized action when applicable → outcome evidence → new experience
```

Experience is not automatically truth. One successful workflow, model response, evaluation, or provider outcome is not automatically a durable rule. Working knowledge must remain provenance-backed, scoped, challengeable, freshness-aware, and supersedable.

## PromptOS-specific boundary

No workflow, mode, registry entry, generated instruction, learned optimization, model consensus, or historical success may self-expand scope or create execution, merge, deployment, publication, provider, financial, or credential authority. Portable instructions must preserve local repository and product authority ceilings.

## Mandatory audit questions

> If the current model, provider, workflow runtime, FCR, or another named dependency disappeared now, what stops, what survives, what degrades safely, and what evidence proves the answer?

> What experience is PromptOS learning from, what would falsify the current rule, and who actually holds authority for the next consequential action?

Architecture docs and workflow registration are not failover, execution, or learning proof. Verify contract, registry/version agreement, focused tests, runtime, authority, data exit, and real-path evidence separately.

Use: Reality → ULTRATHINK → Red Team 1 → Lindy → L99 → OODA → smallest reversible implementation → focused proof → Red Team 2 / provider-loss + knowledge-corruption attack → Documentation Truth → rollback → next gate.
